Research

I develop disease models of retinal degenerative disorders using induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) technology and mouse models, and use them to understand disease mechanisms and identify therapeutic targets. My work in Dr. Ruchira Singh's laboratory focuses on two diseases: CLN3 disease (juvenile Batten disease), a lysosomal storage disorder, and age-related macular degeneration (AMD).

CLN3 disease. The CLN3 protein sits in several cellular compartments, including lysosomes, which digest and recycle cellular molecules, yet its primary function is still unclear. I study how CLN3 mutation leads to mis-trafficking of lysosomal enzymes and altered lysosomal pH.

Age-related macular degeneration. AMD affects the photoreceptor–RPE–choriocapillaris complex and is a major cause of vision loss. Current treatments slow progression but rarely improve vision, carry a risk of conversion to wet AMD, and impose a heavy treatment burden. I have contributed to establishing iPSC-derived RPE (iRPE) models of AMD and related macular dystrophies that reproduce drusen formation, a key hallmark of the disease, and I am using them to dissect how lipid mediators such as PLA2G2A and lysosomal dysfunction trigger early RPE dysfunction.

Retinal lipid metabolism. During my PhD at the L V Prasad Eye Institute, I used multi-omics approaches focused on lipid metabolism to identify the role of arachidonic acid and prostaglandin metabolism in retinal neovascularization and neurodegeneration in retinopathy of prematurity. This work also led me to co-develop non-invasive, tear-based biosensors for early detection of eye disease.
